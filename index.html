PLS 210 · Research Design
PRESENTATION #1

Municipal Elderly-Care Spending
and Youth-Program Funding
in South Korea

A quantitative study of a possible local budget trade-off · 2015–2023

Research Question

RQ

“To what extent did rising municipal spending on elderly care reduce local government funding for youth programs in South Korea between 2015 and 2023?”

Question Design

Empirical · Scoped · Answerable

EmpiricalIt asks what actually happened to public spending, so it can be studied with budget data rather than opinion.
ScopedThe focus is municipal governments in South Korea, specifically elderly care and youth programs.
Time window · 2015–2023This is the period for which standardized, comparable municipal financial data make a consistent panel possible.
AnswerableLocal Finance 365 provides municipal-level spending data for the relevant categories across this window.
Context & Research Position

Positioning & Epistemology

Why municipal governments?
  • They are the local level where spending priorities become actual budget allocations.
  • Municipalities face different demographic and fiscal pressures, creating variation to compare.
  • The study therefore connects population ageing to concrete local budget choices.
Why 2015–2023?
  • The period provides standardized, comparable integrated financial statements across local governments.
  • This makes a consistent municipality-level panel possible.
  • It also captures a period of increasing pressure from population ageing.
EpistemologyPositivist
Budgets are treated as observable and measurable evidence.
DesignQuantitative · Large-N panel
“To what extent” requires measuring relationships across many municipalities.
Why It Matters

Contribution

Why the study matters

Population ageing creates pressure for governments to expand elderly-care spending while municipalities still need to fund programs for younger residents.

Research gap

Existing Korean fiscal studies explain broad changes in local spending, but they do not directly test the specific elderly-care → youth-program relationship.

What this study contributes

It moves the discussion from broad welfare categories to a concrete, measurable intra-welfare budget trade-off.

What remains unclosed

The study does not explain every reason why youth spending changes. Other demographic, political, transfer, and revenue factors may also shape the outcome.

Contribution in one line: testing whether the growth of one age-targeted welfare priority is associated with reduced funding for another.
Literature Review · Branch 1

Lit Review · Decentralization

Cluster 1: Decentralization changes the composition of local budgets, not just their size.
Kwon: As Korea decentralized starting in the 1990s, local governments picked up real discretion while the central government's share shrank.
Park: Using data from 2008–2018, Park found that revenue-side decentralization reduces welfare's share of local budgets and pushes spending from current expenses toward capital investment.
Finding from Branch 1: Decentralization can change which parts of the local budget receive resources.
Literature Review · Branch 2

Lit Review · Political & Institutional Levers

Cluster 2: Political and institutional levers decide which categories move.
Ha & Lee: Politically aligned regions get larger transfers from the center, and those transfers translate into higher social-welfare and education spending. Progressive mayors spend noticeably more on both than conservative ones.
Lee & Min: Participatory budgeting shifts spending toward welfare, health, and transport, and away from development projects.
Key tension: Park finds decentralization shrinking welfare's share, while Ha & Lee and Lee & Min identify specific channels that grow it.
Literature Review · Synthesis

What the Literature Tells Us

Finding 1

Decentralization does not have one automatic effect. Park finds a smaller welfare share, while other studies identify channels that increase welfare spending.

Finding 2

The effect depends on the lever. Transfers, political leadership, and participatory budgeting can move spending in different directions.

Overall: Welfare spending in Korean municipalities does not seem to respond to decentralization as one single force — it depends on which particular lever is being pulled.
Theory

Theory & Mechanism

Mechanism: Elderly-care spending is relatively constrained by welfare obligations and central-government co-financing. Youth-program spending is comparatively more discretionary. If elderly-care spending grows faster than the total budget, pressure may fall on flexible spending lines.
H1: Municipalities with a larger rise in elderly-care spending from 2015–2023 saw a larger fall in youth-program funding.

H0: Changes in elderly-care spending are unrelated to changes in youth-program funding.
Independent variableChange in elderly-care spending, 2015–2023, as a share of the municipal budget.
Dependent variableChange in youth-program funding over the same period.
ControlsRevenue decentralization · mayoral political ideology · participatory budgeting · total budget size.

Reflection

What AI Helped Me Understand

From broad topic → testable mechanism “Ageing affects local budgets” is too broad. The useful question is whether a specific increase in elderly-care spending is connected to a measurable change in another specific budget line.
Why the literature gap matters The existing studies explain how local spending is shaped, but they do not directly examine the elderly-care → youth-program relationship I want to test.
Reflection

What I Still Don’t Understand

What the data will actually show I do not yet know whether the expected budget trade-off will appear consistently across municipalities — and that is something the empirical analysis needs to establish.
How strong the relationship will be Even if the relationship exists, I still need to determine how large it is and whether it remains after accounting for other fiscal and political factors.
Next step: test the proposed mechanism rather than assuming the expected relationship is already established.
